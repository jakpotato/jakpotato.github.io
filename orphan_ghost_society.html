A responsible approach

Ghost welfare comes first.

Not every empty house is suitable. Not every ghost is suited to every residence. We assess temperament, haunting patterns, unfinished business, and the needs of everyone involved.

Important: The Society does not guarantee that a referred ghost is harmless. Some ghosts are merely quiet. Others have been known to move furniture, open cupboards, appear in mirrors, or leave inexplicable shadows.

About the Society

Founded because someone had to look after the dead.

The Orphan Ghost Society was established in 1897 after a group of civic-minded residents noticed an unusual increase in unexplained knocking following the demolition of several Victorian homes.

What began as a small committee for displaced household spirits has grown into an international network of volunteers, hosts, researchers, and people willing to investigate a room that everyone else insists is empty.

Our mission is simple: to ensure that no ghost is left without a safe and appropriate place to reside.

Our principles

Every ghost is considered.
We do not exclude ghosts based on origin, appearance, age, or temperament.

Safety is shared.
Living people, animals, hosts, and ghosts all deserve consideration.

Placement should be sustainable.
We seek residences where a ghost can remain without repeatedly being asked to leave.

Privacy matters.
Some ghosts are surprisingly sensitive about their previous lives.

Resources

Guidance for the living and the recently deceased.

I think there is a ghost in my home. What should I do?

Remain calm and document what you can. Avoid confronting an aggressive entity. If there is an immediate physical safety concern, contact appropriate emergency services. For non-emergency cases, you can submit a referral to the Society.

The ghost is making shadows, but there is nobody there.

Please mention this in your referral. Shadow formation is a documented but inconsistently understood phenomenon among certain cases. Do not assume the ghost is hostile solely because it has developed a strong visual presence.

Can I adopt a ghost permanently?

Permanent placement is handled case-by-case. Some ghosts are appropriate for private residences; others are better suited to historic properties, institutional settings, or specialized arrangements.

What if the ghost refuses to leave?

Do not attempt unsafe or coercive removal. Contact the Society for guidance. A ghost that has remained in one location for decades may have stronger ties to that property than initially understood.

Can animals be ghosts?

Yes. The Society accepts reports involving deceased companion animals and other non-human spirits where appropriate placement services are available.

Can a ghost be too difficult to place?

Some cases require extended assessment. Particularly difficult cases may be marked for specialized placement if they demonstrate persistent poltergeist activity, multiple manifestations, possession claims, or an unexplained ability to operate modern appliances.

For current residents

Living with an existing ghost.

If a ghost has become part of your household and the arrangement is generally peaceful, you may not need to relocate them. Many ghosts are capable of coexisting with living residents when boundaries are established.

Suggested boundaries

• Establish rooms that are off-limits.

• Keep a written record of unusual activity.

• Avoid promising the ghost anything you cannot deliver.

• Do not leave food out unless you have confirmed the ghost actually wants it.
